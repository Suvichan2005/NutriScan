# Local embedding retrieval

Implemented and measured through 22 October 2026 (India time). `POST /api/evidence` accepts `{ "query": "INS 955", "limit": 4 }` with a same-origin localhost request. It returns genuine model cosine scores, lexical BM25 scores, exact identity matches, original source metadata and dated limitations. Returned passages are evidence candidates, never a clinical verdict or proof of current law.

Run `npm run embeddings:prepare` once with internet access, then `npm run embeddings:evaluate`. Preparation downloads approximately 24 MB of Apache-2.0 model/tokenizer assets to ignored `.data/models/`, verifies finite 384-dimensional output, and writes the compact reproducible vector index under `data/`. Runtime queries disable all remote model loading and execute on local Node CPU. No health text or query is sent to Hugging Face. No API key is needed. The server retains a single pipeline and rejects concurrent work while busy; command-line scripts dispose it explicitly.

The pretrained encoder is [Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2](https://huggingface.co/Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2), the Transformers.js ONNX conversion of [sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2](https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2), pinned at revision `751bff37182d3f1213fa05d7196b954e230abad9`. Transformers.js 4.3.1 was measured. Mean pooling and unit normalization follow the model usage. `data/semantic-model-manifest.json` records actual downloaded byte sizes and SHA-256 values. The q8 model itself is 22,972,370 bytes. Runtime does not fabricate vectors or use hand-built concept vectors.

The corpus contains 22 curated entries in `src/modules/knowledge/index.ts`: WHO sodium, non-sugar-sweetener, carbohydrate-quality, free-sugars and trans-fat guidance; NIDDK diabetes meal education; ten FAO/JECFA/Codex additive identity records, including the Codex GSFA lecithin family (INS 322) and amorphous silicon dioxide (INS 551); bibliographic metadata for ICMR-NIN's 2024 Indian Dietary Guidelines; and five FSSAI records covering dated allergen wording, the 2021 industrial-trans-fat amendment, the Advertising and Claims Version IV compendium, and its amendment index. The FSSAI records support generic explanations of dated claim wording; they do not establish a product's values, applicable basis, compliance or current law. Every record has a license/terms note: known restrictions are recorded, while unverified terms are explicitly labeled unknown; this is provenance tracking, not legal clearance. The additive records support identity and synonym lookups only; their source pages do not establish product presence, dose or national authorization. Generic INS 322 does not resolve its Codex subtype. NutriScan does not reproduce the ICMR-NIN recommendations because its notice restricts electronic product use without prior written permission. It stores original short summaries and citation metadata rather than whole source publications; the WHO sugars publication is all-rights-reserved and is linked without reproducing its wording. The two new Codex records' downstream reuse terms were not independently reviewed. Corpus version `2026-10-22.3` and a SHA-256 of source IDs, text and URLs must match the index; source drift fails closed and requires regeneration. Static retrieval never establishes a current Indian legal permission, product dose or personal carbohydrate target.

## Measured developer evaluation

The current 21 developer-authored queries in `data/semantic-evaluation-queries.json` include prior exact terms, additive identifiers, paraphrases, free-sugars and package-versus-population-guidance questions, one FSSAI high-protein-claim query, and two new exact-identity cases for INS 322 and INS 551. Every query has a declared relevant source ID. `data/semantic-evaluation-results.json` contains individual ranks, actual cosine/BM25 scores, timestamps and latency. Lexical and pure semantic scores rank all 22 corpus entries; hybrid exposes five results. Recall@3 means whether the declared relevant source occurs in the first three results.

| Method | Top-1 correct | Recall@3 | MRR@5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lexical BM25 | 20/21 (95.2%) | 20/21 (95.2%) | 0.9524 |
| MiniLM cosine only | 18/21 (85.7%) | 20/21 (95.2%) | 0.8968 |
| Hybrid + exact identity | 21/21 (100%) | 21/21 (100%) | 1.0000 |

Historical 18-source checkpoint: the corpus grew from 16 to 18 entries for trans-fat context, and those same 18 developer-authored queries were rerun without changing their labels. Semantic metrics and hybrid remained unchanged; lexical Top-1 and Recall@3 stayed 17/18, while MRR@5 moved from 0.9556 to 0.9444 because the sodium-paraphrase source shifted from rank 5 to rank 7 within a zero-score tie. A separate developer smoke query, “What does trans fat mean on this label?”, returned WHO first (cosine 0.6639) and the dated FSSAI Gazette second (0.4849); this query is not part of that 18-case benchmark. The separate developer 13-case boundary diagnostic remained 13/13 on its developer-authored cases.

Current 22-source checkpoint: the two new additive identity cases ranked their intended Codex records first in hybrid retrieval. The prior 19-query hybrid set remains 19/19; adding the two exact-identity cases gives 21/21 hybrid Top-1 and Recall@3. These cases were added by the implementation team and are not held-out. The hybrid uses 90% cosine and 10% normalized BM25, with a separate exact-identity boost. All reported evaluations are **development/tuning results**, not an independent held-out improvement claim, clinical accuracy, OCR accuracy, or general web-search accuracy. The corpus remains small and query labels were written by the implementation team; no independent evaluator has judged relevance. Broader corpora and independently authored held-out queries remain necessary.

In the refreshed Next.js API receipt, the first query after process startup took 2.082 seconds and two warm queries took 12 and 18 ms on this machine. These are local desktop measurements, not a mobile performance guarantee. Remote Gemini, current-law research and genuine-photo OCR acceptance are separate integration gates.

The earlier real localhost Next.js checks in `data/semantic-api-receipt.json` recorded an FSSAI allergen query, exact INS 955, a blood-pressure paraphrase, hostile-Origin rejection and invalid-bound rejection at corpus version `2026-10-21.8`; these remain historical API evidence. The local model assets matched their recorded hashes before this index regeneration; no model download or remote provider call occurred. The refreshed mobile/desktop browser receipt checks the additive identity cards, guideline summaries, query abstention and limitations. Browser counts and current screenshots are recorded separately in `docs/evidence/local-evidence-browser.json`; an HTTP receipt verifies the local integration path, not external provider acceptance.

Known limitations: a tiny English corpus, no calibrated out-of-domain rejection, model token truncation, unknown source supersession, no independent annotation, and no freshness guarantee. Unrelated queries can still retrieve weakly similar passages. The response explicitly reports this limitation; it must not turn a top-ranked passage into a supported answer without checking applicability.

## Original 0.30 floor and intent guard

A separate 13-query diagnostic is recorded after the original ranking evaluation. It includes five unrelated requests, six supported or scope-boundary questions and two current-law queries. Before adding a guard, the unrelated requests scored 0.10–0.16 on the hybrid scale, while the four original supported examples scored 0.34–0.65. A current ORS-law query scored 0.40 against the ICMR bibliography, showing that similarity alone could surface an irrelevant dietary record for a legal question.

At this historical checkpoint, the endpoint suppressed candidates below a 0.30 development score floor and routed queries containing common regulatory terms (including ORS, FSSAI and Gazette) to a no-match response before model inference. In the original 13-case run, all five general out-of-domain and both current-law queries returned no local candidates; all six supported/scope-boundary probes returned an expected source. The 0.30 floor was later increased after new false matches were reproduced. The original browser receipt remains useful for its then-current acceptance behavior but is superseded by the current receipt below.

## Current 0.40 floor and explicit identifier lookup

Before the current change, two developer-authored probes retrieved unrelated material: “Does beta glucan in oats affect blood cholesterol?” returned NIDDK meal guidance at hybrid score 0.3966, and the shorter “beta glucan oats” query returned WHO carbohydrate guidance at 0.3012. The uncalibrated local candidate floor is now 0.40, so both queries abstain. This addresses observed near-floor false associations while preserving useful results in the existing authored cases; it is not a calibrated relevance probability.

The hybrid endpoint now also recognizes exactly one prefixed additive identifier inside a natural-language question. “What does INS 322 mean on a food label?” resolves the attributed FAO/Codex lecithin-family record; multiple distinct codes do not receive an arbitrary exact boost. On the same 21 developer-authored ranking cases, lexical, semantic and hybrid metrics remain unchanged, with hybrid 21/21. The updated 16-case boundary set meets all expected outcomes: seven source matches and nine abstentions, including the two beta-glucan probes, current-law questions, unrelated queries and the natural-language INS 322 case. These are implementation-authored cases; no independent user-query relevance study or OCR measurement is represented.

The updated Chrome/Playwright flow exercised the real `/api/evidence` route at 390×844 and 1440×900. Each viewport made nine same-origin requests, all HTTP 200; the natural-language INS 322 query displayed the correct attributed record, and the beta-glucan question showed the no-match state. A separate conversational flow with synthetic typed-label text returned the NIDDK summary with its US applicability limits, then withheld sources for both an unrelated question and the same beta-glucan question; three evidence requests per viewport returned HTTP 200. Both flows had zero Axe violations, page errors or off-loopback requests, and the conversation flow made no assist/vision requests. This verifies typed-query integration with the local corpus only; it does not measure photographic extraction, independent relevance or Gemini behavior. Receipts and ignored screenshots: `docs/evidence/local-evidence-browser.json`, `docs/evidence/conversational-evidence-browser.json` and `docs/evidence/private/`.

This is a conservative guard tested on developer-authored probes, not a calibrated relevance probability or an independent query benchmark. Novel legal wording and unrelated questions that score above the floor may still retrieve weak matches. Current-law verification remains in the separate dated regulatory workflow.
